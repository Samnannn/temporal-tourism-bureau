Section 03 — The advisory board

Some centuries
arrive with conditions.

Six advisories are standing. Three of them concern your own behaviour, which the Board has found is the leading cause of incident — ahead of weather, wildlife and the year 1786.

Board of Chronological Safety

Last revised 12 October 2087. The Board meets on Thursdays; ratings may change without notice and usually do. Advisories apply to every traveller regardless of class.

Advisory line +00 (TT) 14-B

Section 01 — The scale Reg. 14 / 2087

Four levels.
One is a warning about you.

Level 1 means nothing in the century will hurt you. Level 4 means the Board has written a second form and will read it aloud if necessary.

Level 01 Routine

Nothing in the era is likely to injure you. Conditions apply only because the Bureau has learned not to leave travellers unsupervised.

No extra paperwork
e.g. 2041
Level 02 Caution

The century is safe; the people in it are particular. Etiquette, customs and family are the three most common causes of an incident report.

Acknowledged at visa
e.g. 1605 · 1786 · 537
Level 03 High

Real risk of injury, illness or a change you cannot undo. Guides hold discretionary authority to end the visit early. They use it.

Signed waiver 3
e.g. 250 BCE · 9,500 BCE
Level 04 Extreme

Conditions can kill a healthy adult in under a minute. Group size is capped, the route is fixed, and the field screen is not a suggestion.

Waiver 4 + briefing
e.g. 68M BCE

Ratings describe the era, not the itinerary. A Level 4 era can be visited safely — thousands of travellers have — by following instructions written by people who did not.

Section 02 — Standing advisories Six on file

The register.
Open, read, acknowledge.

Every advisory below is acknowledged individually during the visa application. Selecting "I have read this" is a legal act, not a formality.

01-A Customs: what may cross a corridor Level 2
Customs

A corridor is not a suitcase. Everything entering a century is assessed for what the Bureau calls chronological weight: the chance that an object changes a decision, and the chance that the decision changes a century. Most objects are fine. Three are not.

  1. Declare anything with a date on it — newspapers, tickets, letters, photographs, and coins still in circulation where you are going.
  2. Declare anything that will be invented in the destination century. It will be recognised, and you will be asked where you got it.
  3. Hand over all unopened correspondence. It is returned to you afterwards, unopened, which is more than the recipient managed.
  4. Do not declare your phone. It will be held at the desk and you will receive a stamped chit. Complaining about this counts as a fourth item.

Issued 04/2086 · Revised 12/2087 · Inspection mandatory, both directions

03-A The Cretaceous: do not leave the field screen Level 4
Deep time

The field screen is a licensed exclusion zone twelve metres in radius, rated for forty-eight megafauna encounters per season. It is not a fence. It is a suggestion with a legal department.

No fatalities have been recorded on this itinerary. Six near-misses have, all of them photographed from inside the screen, which is why the photographs survive.

  1. Stay inside the screen. The picnic is catered; there is no reason to go and look at anything.
  2. Do not bring food, and do not carry food on your person. You are, biologically, food.
  3. Do not photograph the horizon unattended. The horizon is not the problem; your attention is.
  4. If a guide says "walk, do not run", walk. Running is what makes you interesting.
  5. Return on the whistle. There is one whistle, and it is not tested twice.

Issued 06/2087 · Revised 07/2087 · Group size capped at 9 · Waiver 4 required

14-B Addressing relatives you have not met yet Level 3
Conduct

The most common incident on the register is not a paradox. It is a family lunch. Meeting an ancestor is permitted. Being recognised by one is a Level 3 event, and being fed by one is a Level 3 event with additional paperwork.

  1. Do not use your surname in a house where it is already in use.
  2. Do not correct a relative's memory of an event you were not yet born for. You will be right, and it will not help.
  3. If you are offered food, accept one item and finish it. Refusing is remembered for longer than you are.
  4. Do not photograph anyone who asks why. In most centuries, the honest answer ends the visit.
  5. Leave before the second cup of tea. The second cup is where the questions begin.

Issued 02/2085 · Revised 04/2087 · Applies to all genealogical itineraries

22-C The Quiet of 2041 Level 1
Routine

This era is rated Level 1 because nothing in it will hurt you. It is rated at all because the Bureau has learned that travellers will fill a silence if left unsupervised.

  1. Bring nothing with a battery. There is nothing to charge it with, and nothing to say to it.
  2. Arrive at 15:40. The table is booked under your family's name, which is not your name.
  3. Sit down. Do not introduce yourself. You are not a guest; you are the afternoon.
  4. Do not console anyone. It is not a sad hour. It is the last ordinary one.
  5. Leave at 17:20, before the networks return. The return is loud, and you will want to be gone.

Issued 08/2087 · Revised 09/2087 · Duration fixed at one afternoon

27-F Currency, trade and the going rate for a coin Level 3
Trade

The Bureau issues era coinage at the corridor desk, stamped and weighed for the century you are entering. It is not a souvenir. Spending it is fine; keeping it is a customs matter with a short and unfriendly name.

  1. Use only Bureau coinage. A coin from the wrong century is the fastest way to be remembered.
  2. Do not accept change unless you can name the metal by sight. If you cannot, hand it back and smile.
  3. Haggling is expected in 1605, impolite in 1786, and a form of theatre in Alexandria. Your guide will indicate which is in progress.
  4. Do not buy anything with a portrait on it, in either direction. Portraits are people, and people are paperwork.
  5. Tipping is not practised before 1700 and is effectively compulsory at the Mughal court, in the sense that refusing becomes a story about you.

Issued 11/2086 · Revised 08/2087 · Exchange at the corridor desk only

31-D Medical events in the past tense Level 3
Medical

Bureau doctors can treat you in any century. They cannot always treat you with the medicine of that century, because it has not been invented, and they will not explain why they are washing their hands.

  1. Carry your own course. The Bureau supplies protection against eleven extinct diseases and a card reading, in four languages, "I am not a spy, I am a patient."
  2. Do not accept treatment from a local physician. They are frequently excellent and always working from a different theory of the body.
  3. If you are injured, return immediately. A scar you bring back is a Level 3 incident. A scar you leave behind is a Level 4.
  4. Report any new allergy within 24 hours of return. The Bureau keeps a record for a reason it will explain after you report it.
  5. Do not die abroad. The paperwork is substantial, and the funeral is complicated by the fact that you are not yet born.

Section 03 — Protocol Form 88 / 2087

If you meet yourself,
the meeting is already going badly.

Forty-one self-encounters are on record. None were fatal. Nine ended in a change of career, usually toward the civil service. The protocol below is short because there is no time.

Form 88 · Immediate action

Read this before departure. Recite it once at the corridor desk. You will not remember it precisely, and that is why we make you recite it.

No action requiredSelf-encounter
not yet reported
  1. Do not shake hands. You will both remember it differently, and one of you will be correct.
  2. Say nothing about the future. Even "you look well" is information. Especially "you look well".
  3. Leave within four minutes. The corridor is booked for five. The extra minute is a courtesy to you, not to them.
  4. If they speak first, agree with everything. Do not improve their sentence. Do not finish it.
  5. Report it to your guide. Not to the other you, who will want the details and will use them.
  6. If it happens twice, stop travelling. We refund the balance and do not ask questions, which is itself unusual.
Section 04 — The customs hall Desk 2 · Level −2

What travels.
What stays behind.

Officers inspect at both ends of the corridor. Declare anything with a date on it, anything not yet invented, and anything you would rather not explain to a customs officer in 1605.

May cross

Desk 2
  • Bureau coinage, stamped and weighed for the destination century.
  • Clothing of the period, supplied and fitted at the corridor desk.
  • One printed photograph of your own family, if you have one.
  • Letters of introduction, sealed and dated by the Bureau.
  • Medication in its original Bureau-issued case, with the green docket.
  • A guide. Always a guide.

May not cross

No exceptions
  • Anything with an active network connection, including watches.
  • Anything invented after your destination year.
  • Living plants, animals or seeds. The Bureau does not argue about these.
  • Unopened correspondence addressed to anyone but you.
  • Weapons of any century, including the one you are going to.
  • More than one photograph of the same person.
Section 05 — Medical desk Effective 01/2087

Inoculations
and the anti-nostalgia course.

All courses are included in the fare and administered at the corridor desk. The Bureau will not take you to a century that predates your medication. This is not negotiable.

Course Required for Schedule Notes
Anti-nostalgia All eras 2 tablets · 12 h before Most travellers cry once, briefly, at the airport. This is expected and not covered by insurance.
Extinct pathogen panel Any era before 1500 1 injection · 14 days prior Covers plague, pox and one fever the Bureau no longer names in print.
Chronological disorientation All eras 1 tablet · on return Do not drive, sign documents or answer family questions for six hours.
Parasite screen Cretaceous · First Autumn 2 doses · pre and post You will be scanned at length. The scanning is not a judgement, though it feels like one.
Temporal immunity booster 2041 · 3000 1 booster · 72 h prior You will not notice it working. That is the intended experience.
Language prophylaxis Optional · all eras 3 sessions · 1 h each Not a substitute for a guide, and not a substitute for listening to one.

If you are unwell before departure

Travel is postponed, not cancelled. The Bureau will hold your place, your fare and your era for up to ninety days. After ninety days the era may be re-surveyed without you, which is a polite way of saying the century will have moved on.

If you are unwell during a visit

Tell your guide immediately. Do not wait for the scheduled return, and do not accept a remedy you cannot identify. The medical desk operates in every open era and answers within four minutes, which is faster than most centuries can send for help.

Before you apply

Read, acknowledge,
then go.

Each advisory is acknowledged separately at the visa stage. If you have already booked, the acknowledgements are printed on your manifest and cannot be removed, which is the point of printing them.
