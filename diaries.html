Section 07 — The returned

Notes from people
who came back.

The Bureau Review publishes traveller diaries exactly as filed, including the parts that contradict the official record. Volume XI, 2087. Seven entries, none of them improved.

The Bureau Review

Diaries are held for ninety years or until the traveller dies, whichever happens first, whichever direction. Names are used with permission, which is not always obtained in the same century.

Volume XI · 2087

Diary 001The Last Connected Afternoon · 2041

Four minutes in the wrong kitchen

Nour Haddad spent one afternoon at a table that was not hers. She has been trying to describe it for eight years, and the Bureau has been trying to stop her.

Read the diary

We arrived at 15:40 and the table was already laid. Four cups, one teapot, a bowl of pears that nobody touched. The guide told me the booking was under my family's name, which is not my name, and that I should sit down and not introduce myself. I have never been so visible while saying nothing.

One of them looked up once, at the window. That was the whole event. I have spent eight years trying to explain why it undid me: four people in a kitchen, all of them somewhere else, and the afternoon holding them anyway.

At 17:20 the networks came back and the room filled with sound. The guide put a hand on my shoulder. We left before anyone could ask who I was. I have not looked at my phone at dinner since. I am writing this eight years later and it is still true.

NHNour HaddadTeacher · travelled 2103
Diary 002Alexandria · 250 BCE

The library smelled of wet wool and cedar

An archivist goes to see the catalogue she has spent her career reconstructing from footnotes.

Read the diary

I expected marble and got a working building: wet wool, cedar oil, and the particular impatience of people carrying too many scrolls. Nobody whispered. The librarian made me wait while she finished an argument about a comma, then asked what I had brought.

I had brought blank papyrus, as instructed. She looked at it for a long time and said, "A gift with no opinion in it. Good." I was allowed to stand in the catalogue room for eleven minutes and not read anything, which was the hardest instruction I have ever followed.

ISIra SenArchivist · travelled 2086
Diary 003The Mughal Court · 1605

Thirty-seven painters, one lamp

A conservator is permitted to watch the atelier finish a manuscript she will one day be forbidden to touch.

Read the diary

They worked in shifts around a single north-facing lamp, and the room was quieter than any museum I have worked in. One painter held a page by its corner for an hour so the gold could dry without a mark. Nobody spoke to him. Nobody needed to.

I was told not to give an opinion and gave one anyway, about a border. The head painter considered it, thanked me, and did the opposite. I have never been more relieved to be overruled.

RBRohan BhattConservator · travelled 2084
Diary 004The Cretaceous · 68M BCE

We were told not to look at the horizon

A field medic on the picnic, and the four minutes she still refuses to describe in detail.

Read the diary

The air was so rich it felt like a second language. We ate under the screen and I counted birds that were not birds. The guide kept saying "walk, do not run", which sounded like a joke until the second hour.

Something large crossed the river at a distance and everyone stopped chewing. I looked at the table, not the horizon, as instructed. The table was shaking. So was I. We left on the whistle and nobody needed to be told twice.

PNPriya NwosuField medic · travelled 2081
Diary 005Hagia Sophia · 537

The dome held. So did we.

A stone mason watches the building she has repaired in the present, on the day it was first finished.

Read the diary

I have spent nine years replacing stones in that dome. Standing under it in 537, with the scaffolding still up and the emperor still deciding whether to look pleased, I understood that I had been working on a rumour. The building was much louder than the rumour.

We were told not to speak during the liturgy, and I did not, but I cried in a way that felt structural. The verger gave me a piece of bread afterwards and asked no questions. I have kept the crumbs in a tin. Do not tell customs.

LMLuca MarchettiStone mason · travelled 2079
Diary 006The Quiet Millennium · 3000

A morning in the year 3000, doing nothing correctly

A civil servant goes to see what the job looks like when it has been done properly for a thousand years.

Read the diary

Nobody hurried. That was the first thing, and it took me three days to stop noticing it. The canals are clear because they are cleaned, not because someone wrote a policy about cleaning them. The buildings breathe. The forms are two pages.

I asked a clerk what happens when something goes wrong. She thought about it and said, "It is noticed early." I have written that on a card above my desk in 2087. It has not made my department any faster, but it has made me calmer about it.

AKAnanya KrishnanCivil servant · travelled 2085
Diary 007The First Autumn · 9,500 BCE

The first autumn, and the last apple

A chef brings salt to a household that has never tasted it, and is invited to stay for the season.

Read the diary

I brought salt because it was traditional and because a chef likes to arrive with something. They tasted it, looked at each other, and then looked at me in a way I have only seen in kitchens: the look of people recalculating a menu.

The last apple of the season was split four ways. I was given a quarter and told to eat it slowly. It was the best thing I have ever eaten and I have run a restaurant for nineteen years. I did not bring anything back, because the rules are clear and the valley is not a pantry.

JOJide OkonkwoChef · travelled 2083
