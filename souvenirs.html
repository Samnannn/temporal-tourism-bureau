Section 06 — Authorised goods

Nothing that remembers
where it has been.

The Bureau shop sells objects that survived the corridor and can prove it. Everything here is a replica, an extract or a certified original. Nothing here can be called as a witness.

Corridor 4, beside the lost property window. Open whenever the present is. Purchases are held at the gate and handed over after customs, which is the only part of the transaction anybody remembers.

Section 02 — Certification Reg. 22 / 2087

How an object becomes
safe to bring home.

The object store is the least visited room in the Bureau and the only one with a waiting list. Four stages, no shortcuts, and a stamp that is cut rather than printed.

01 —

Sourced

Recovered, catalogued, and in most cases returned to the exact shelf it came from. The shop's records are more complete than the century's, and considerably more honest.

02 —

Tested

Eleven days in the inertness chamber. If it ticks, glows, remembers a name or improves anything, it is destroyed and the paperwork is filed under "learning".

03 —

Stamped

The replica mark is cut into the base, not printed on it. Removing the mark voids the object's status first and your return chit second. Customs checks in that order.

04 —

Carried

Collected at the gate on your return and declared at the desk. The declaration form is short. The answer is already printed on the base of the object.

Section 03 — Customs Applies in both directions

What leaves with you.
What stays where it was.

A souvenir is an object that has agreed to stop being evidence. The two lists below are read aloud at the corridor desk, in this order, to every traveller.

May cross

Cleared
  • Replicas bearing an intact replica mark
  • Objects with no date, no portrait and no mechanism
  • One item per traveller, per era, per lifetime
  • Letters you wrote yourself and did not send
  • Dust, if it is sealed in a labelled vial
  • A receipt. Always the receipt.

Will be held

Not cleared
  • Anything that remembers
  • Coins still in circulation where you found them
  • Correspondence in someone else's hand
  • Objects from the century you are going to
  • Anything that ticks, hums or improves
  • The receipt, if the object is not on it

After the counter

Buy nothing
that can testify.

Souvenirs are packed by the corridor desk and travel in the coat. Your visa, the standing advisories and a seven-kilogram allowance are the rest of the paperwork, and the only parts that can delay you.
